# OffMenu scanner engineering decision — 12 October 2026

## Decision

Default new devices to front-camera screen-up bench mode, matching the requested checkout workflow. Recommend a rear camera for handheld scanning. For hands-free use, the preferred physical arrangement is a stable stand with the rear lens exposed and audible confirmation enabled. Offer front-camera screen-up bench mode as a supported alternative, with mirrored preview by default and a clear distance/focus explanation. There is no universally best camera configuration across phones. Browser capability detection and a physical-device evaluation are essential.

A phone flat on the table, screen-up, physically obscures its rear lens. Front cameras may be fixed-focus or have a longer near-focus distance. Moving the product farther away can restore sharpness but makes its barcode occupy fewer pixels. Software contrast processing cannot recover detail lost to severe defocus or motion blur. Suggested 15–30 cm rear / 30–50 cm front starting distances are setup heuristics, not measured limits for the user's phone.

## Research and useful product patterns

- Google's ML Kit guide recommends adequate sampling (roughly two pixels per narrowest bar; an EAN-13 spans 95 modules), 720p/1080p acquisition, format restriction, repeated agreeing reads, and dropping stale frames. This informs our web implementation; ML Kit's native Android auto-zoom is not a web feature we claim to provide. [Google guide](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/barcode-scanning/android)
- Scandit's camera API explains that camera metadata may be incomplete and automatic camera choice is heuristic. We therefore expose the actual available lens list rather than claiming the default rear lens is always optimal. [Scandit camera API](https://docs.scandit.com/data-capture-sdk/web/core/api/camera.html)
- Scandit's scanning workflows combine continuous scanning, feedback, duplicate filtering and deliberate target selection. These are relevant UX patterns. Its commercial SDK remains an evaluation candidate; vendor performance claims do not establish superiority on OffMenu's devices. No commercial licence or paid service has been activated. [Scandit workflow](https://docs.scandit.com/sdks/web/single-scanning/)
- Dedicated scanners use stands and presentation mode for the same hands-free task. Zebra's DS2200 family illustrates this pattern. OffMenu now submits a keyboard-wedge scan ending in Enter from the barcode input; actual USB/Bluetooth pairing remains device-dependent. [Zebra DS2200](https://www.zebra.com/ap/en/products/scanners/general-purpose-handheld-scanners/ds2200-series.html)
- W3C defines focus, exposure, torch and zoom constraints, but browsers and hardware expose different subsets. Absence of focus controls is not proof that a camera has no autofocus. Controls are shown only when reported by the track. [Image Capture specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/image-capture/)
- ZXing-C++ WebAssembly supplies an open-source cross-browser decoder. Rotation, local binarisation and harder decoding are configurable. Our test found that `tryInvert` alone did not recover an inverted EAN-13, so the implementation explicitly retries inverted pixels. [ZXing WASM](https://github.com/Sec-ant/zxing-wasm), [reader options](https://sec-ant.github.io/zxing-wasm/docs/interfaces/full.ReaderOptions.html)

## Implemented

1. Handheld/rear, bench/front, and stand/rear presets; explicit lens selection after permission. Device-local preferences retain camera, mode, mirror, rotation and contrast choices. They are deliberately not cloud-synchronised to a different phone.
2. Preview-only horizontal mirroring and 180° rotation. Decoder pixels always come from the untransformed source. Barcode rotation and white-on-black inversion are separate decoding operations.
3. Preferred 1920×1080 / 30 fps acquisition, with soft constraints and an OverconstrainedError retry. Continuous focus, exposure and white balance are requested only when advertised. Manual focus, focus-once, zoom, exposure compensation and continuous torch controls appear only when supported.
4. Full visible video frame, without object-fit cropping. A visual guide helps retain the entire barcode and quiet zones. Autofocus limitations and glare guidance stay visible. No fabricated sharpness score or blind auto-zoom.
5. Native BarcodeDetector fast path when it supports all four configured retail formats. A bundled, same-origin ZXing-C++ WASM decoder in a Web Worker handles fallback and unsupported browsers. No CDN dependency at scan time; no frame queue; only fresh video frames are processed. Processing stops on page hide or navigation and late camera acquisitions are immediately released.
6. Raw-pixel decode first; explicit inversion second; optional contrast stretch and its inverse after failure. Rotation is enabled. This is conventional preprocessing, not AI reconstruction or OCR inference.
7. EAN-13, EAN-8, UPC-A and UPC-E camera recognition. UPC-E expands to UPC-A; zero-prefixed EAN-13 normalises to equivalent UPC-A. Checksum and format lengths are validated. Non-retail formats remain available through manual entry, not automatically accepted as products.
8. Two agreeing fresh frames for live acceptance. Multiple decoded product identifiers suppress acceptance. A held code is latched; time passing between slow decoder calls does not itself rearm it. Four empty frames spanning at least 1.2 seconds rearm; an explicit identical-pack button also rearms. Long recognition failure can still resemble removal: physical testing must measure accidental duplicates.
9. Existing optional beep, vibration, glow, torch pulse and spoken product name remain. Starting the scanner resumes previously enabled audio within the user gesture. A screen wake lock is requested where supported. Continuous torch and pulse restore the original track setting.
10. Local barcode-photo fallback with a 20 MB file limit, 2560-pixel processing bound, and one-code rule. It uses the camera app's captured image but cannot guarantee that image is in focus. A photo is a deliberate single submission, so live temporal consensus does not apply.
11. Offline shell includes the decoder worker and WASM. Camera frames/photos are not uploaded, persisted or included in analytics. Only accepted barcode numbers enter existing product lookup and receipt handling.

## Verification

The scanner changes were reconciled with the global data and bring-your-own-credentials branch; 65 automated tests pass, including the existing app tests and 16 new scanner checks. Independent EAN-13 pixel fixtures verify real WASM decoding of upright, 90°, 180°, 270°, mirrored, inverted and low-contrast inputs. Additional checks cover checksum rejection, UPC-E expansion, UPC/EAN normalisation, ambiguous codes, stale-frame duplicate prevention, explicit rearming, blank images, camera permission denial, stop-during-permission races, camera switching, unsupported controls and visibility cleanup. These tests are synthetic and mocked where hardware is involved; they do not establish real phone scanning speed or success rate.

The production bundle builds; its served WASM bytes compile and the JS/WASM response types and CSP permission are checked. Local browser visual QA and physical camera testing are unavailable in this environment. No real-device performance number is claimed.

## Physical acceptance before declaring the scanner dependable

Use the user's Android phone plus at least one iPhone/Safari device. For each supported mode, test real EAN/UPC packages, small labels, glossy/curved packages, dim and bright light, tilted/rotated labels, two identical packs and rapid sequences of different products. Record time to first accepted read, success within two seconds, false reads, duplicate additions, actual lens/resolution/focus capability and chosen working distance. Also check camera switching, permission denial, screen lock, background/foreground, offline restart and spoken feedback.

Proposed product targets (not measured results): zero incorrect identifiers or unintended duplicate additions in the acceptance set, and at least 95% of ordinary undamaged codes accepted within two seconds under normal indoor light. Test harder labels separately. If front bench mode fails, recommend a rear-camera stand, not stronger sharpening claims. If the free decoder misses targets, benchmark Scandit and Dynamsoft on exactly the same labelled samples/devices before selecting a licensed SDK. A native CameraX/ML Kit integration is a later option if browser camera-control limits dominate.
